What makes a bad subway day?

At 9:18 a.m. on February 16, the TTC logged a weather delay at Eglinton. The entry lists 807 minutes. How much can one row change the picture of an entire year?

Kenny Nguyen and Justin Dao

February 16, 2025 · 09:18 807 min Eglinton Station Weather reports and related problems

Most entries last five minutes or less.

Start with all 9,094 subway records that list a positive delay in 2025. About six in ten fall in the shortest group. Just 212 list a delay of half an hour or more.

Now add up the minutes.

The longest group makes up just 2.3% of records. Those same rows account for 20.7% of recorded delay minutes. That makes a small group of entries worth a closer look.

Weather moves up six places.

Rank all 117 incident codes by how often they appear and weather comes in 14th. Rank them by total recorded minutes and weather moves into the top eight. Disorderly patron stays first on both lists.

Three entries on one Sunday.

February 16 accounts for 2,075 of 2,745 recorded weather minutes that year. The three longest entries all came that day. Together they add up to 1,964 minutes. The median weather entry across the year was three minutes.

These durations can overlap. To figure out how long a rider at Eglinton waited, we would need to know when they arrived on the platform and when a train came.

Three rows move weather from 23rd to 8th.

Set those three entries aside and weather drops to 781 minutes. Every other code keeps its full-year total. Use the switch to see how far the weather point moves. You can also explore how the picture changes by month or line.

Data and scope

The charts use Toronto’s 2025 TTC Subway Delay Data and incident code descriptions. They include rows with positive Min Delay and exclude eight rows labelled outside the subway scope in the source. Rows with an unspecified line appear under “Unspecified.” Equal totals share a rank.
